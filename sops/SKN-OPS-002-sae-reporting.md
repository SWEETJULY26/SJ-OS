# Serious Adverse Event (SAE) Reporting and Management

> **Overdue for annual review** as of this migration (due 2026-06-30).

## 1. Purpose

Establish a standardized process for the identification, documentation, investigation, and management of Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) associated with the use of skincare products.

## 2. Scope

All employees and contractors involved in the development, production, and monitoring of skincare products at Sweet July Beauty, LLC.

## 3. Definitions

- **Serious Adverse Event (SAE)** — an adverse event resulting in death, life-threatening conditions, hospitalization, disability, or any significant health event requiring medical intervention.
- **Adverse Event (AE)** — any undesirable experience associated with the use of a product that may or may not be serious.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **Quality Assurance (QA) or designee** — oversees the SAE reporting system, ensures compliance with this SOP, maintains SAE records.
- **Product Development Manager** — leads the investigation of SAEs, ensures appropriate actions are taken.
- **All Employees** — report any SAEs immediately to their supervisor or QA.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Identification and Reporting

Any employee identifying an SAE reports it immediately to their supervisor or QA. An SAE Report Form (Appendix A) is completed with the event details, date, and responsible person.

### 5.2 Documentation

All relevant information is accurately documented on the SAE Report Form: product involved, description of the SAE, patient demographics where applicable, and medical treatment required.

### 5.3 Initial Assessment

QA reviews the SAE Report Form to determine severity and potential impact, and initiates immediate corrective actions if necessary to mitigate further risk. This SOP does not define a formal severity triage tree — severity at this step is a documented judgment call, made and recorded at the time of assessment, not derived from an invented rubric.

### 5.4 Investigation

An investigation team forms, including the Product Safety Officer and relevant personnel, and conducts a thorough investigation to identify the root cause using an appropriate tool (Fishbone Diagram, 5 Whys — Appendix C). Findings are documented. A confirmed root cause routes into the CAPA process per SKN-OPS-001.

### 5.5 Corrective and Preventive Action Plan

Corrective action addresses the root cause; preventive action addresses recurrence. Both are implemented per the defined plan, monitored, and documented to completion — mechanically, this plan is the same CAPA plan structure defined in SKN-OPS-001 §5.3.

### 5.6 Communication and Reporting

**Internal** — findings and actions are communicated to relevant stakeholders.

**External** — reported to regulatory authorities in accordance with local regulations if required, with detailed reports provided as mandated. MoCRA SAE filing mechanics (classification criteria, Pedrero sign-off, statutory clock, agency submission channel) are specified in SKN-OPS-009.

### 5.7 Verification and Effectiveness

QA verifies all actions were completed as planned, confirms the corrective actions effectively addressed the root cause, and documents an effectiveness review.

### 5.8 Documentation and Records

A file is maintained for each SAE: SAE Report Forms, investigation reports, action plans, verification records, effectiveness reviews. Retention: minimum duration per applicable regulatory standard (see §9 below — this SOP left the exact duration unspecified).

## 6. Training

All employees in the SAE process receive appropriate training on this SOP. Training records are documented.

## 7. Review and Approval

This SOP is reviewed annually and updated as necessary. All revisions are approved by the QA Manager.

## 8. Appendices

- **Appendix A** — SAE Report Form.
- **Appendix B** — SAE Investigation Template.
- **Appendix C** — Root Cause Analysis Tools (Fishbone Diagram, 5 Whys).
