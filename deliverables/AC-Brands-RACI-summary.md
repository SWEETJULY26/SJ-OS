# AC Brands RACI, where accountability sits

Prepared for Danielle Iturbe and Nicole Iturbe. Alvin Belt, 6 August 2026.
Reflects the leadership review of 30 July, the role corrections of 31 July, and my own pass over the matrix on 6 August. It covers the whole business rather than only the parts with a skill behind them.

The matrix covers 94 activities across eleven functions: Product Development, Operations & Supply Chain, Quality, Regulatory & Compliance, Retail & Wholesale, Marketing & Brand, Creative, Ecommerce & DTC, Finance, People & Admin, and IT / Systems & Data. It leads with positions rather than people, so it survives a change in who holds a seat. Every row names who answers for the outcome, who does the work, and where that came from: an operating procedure, a job description, or the review itself.

The team-facing version is a web page with the same content, filterable by position, and every activity opens a description with a worked example: **https://claude.ai/code/artifact/e0362bd1-d469-44f2-84d6-9fa5d8c9c9d3**

## What the 6 August pass changed

Accountability on the quality system's own rows moved to me: CAPA lifecycle, vendor quality flags, SOP ratification, the quality dashboard, and complaint trend analysis. Nicole keeps the cross-function final check and I run the system underneath it. That is a change from what we agreed on 30 July and it needs one decision from you, which is the first item under what I still need.

The eight deleted rows were the competitive intelligence block (four rows), three Finance rows on margin pressure-testing and the walk-away call and SKU archetype designation, and retail channel launch programs.

Across both open seats, 43 rows transition on hire, 21 to the Operations Specialist and 22 to the PD Specialist. Thirty-six of those come off me and six off Nicole. My share of the work drops from 55 rows to 19. Rows where one person is both accountable and responsible drop from 44 to 20.

## Quality

Thirteen rows. I answer for seven, Nicole for five, Perrine for one.

Nicole holds the final quality check on product and documentation across every function. Each function still owns its own work; the gate sits on top of it rather than replacing it. That is the piece from the 30 July review that survives unchanged, and it is the row the whole design rests on.

The monthly quality-trend review is what makes the gate worth having. One complaint tells us nothing; a pattern across a quarter tells us whether to reopen a packaging decision. The pump issue is the live example. Target is end of Q3.

## Perrine advises rather than owns

She answers for three rows where the judgment is genuinely formulation: formula stage-gate progression, in-market stability testing, and the close on PD-linked goods receipts. She is consulted on 34, more than anyone except Nicole. Everything process-shaped is consult only: CAPA lifecycle, batch hold and release, vendor quality flags, lab findings, SOP ratification.

This is the split Danielle was pointing at in the review. There were things we had assumed Perrine would manage or organize that she does not, and leadership needs to be able to pull the plug even when she has signed off. Separating technical judgment from process ownership is what that looks like written down.

One consequence of the 6 August pass to look at: pre-launch compatibility, stability, RIPT and PET now has Perrine doing the work with Danielle answering for it. That puts a launch-gating technical call in front of the President rather than inside the contractor relationship, which may be what you want, but it separates the call from the accountability. Same shape on packaging development, where Ayesha now answers for it and Erin and Jan are informed rather than accountable.

## Creative and brand

## Ops Specialist first, and the matrix shows why

I do the work on 11 of the 13 Operations rows. The Operations Specialist job description covers almost exactly that list: daily order operations and the 3PL relationship, channel operations across the store and Ulta Beauty Marketplace and Amazon, inventory reconciliation and FEFO and out-of-stock signals, receiving, purchase-to-pay, freight and customs and brokers, routing-guide and ASN compliance, S&OP support, production scheduling, and the label and PR seeding and sampling work. That is a defined function currently sitting on one person.

The PD Specialist is the seat that closes the quality loop, which is why it comes second rather than never. Its description names running the quality system outright, plus document control and product regulatory work. Until it is filled, Nicole gates quality and I execute it. That works, and it is the reason the gate is worth standing up now instead of waiting, but it is two people covering a role written as one.

Both roles report to Nicole. That was the point of the redesign: moving day-to-day management off the VP seat is the succession outcome, not a reporting-line detail.

## Our external partners are on it

Pedrero Regulatory, Ironclad Finance, Calm HR, WITHIN, Teknologics and Coastal Interactive are all columns on the matrix. They hold the work; accountability stays with one of us on every row.

Finance and HR were never actually unowned. Ironclad runs AP, bookkeeping, payroll, close and reporting, and Calm HR runs handbook, benefits, onboarding, offboarding and separations as our co-employer. They were unowned in our documentation, which is a different problem and one I have now fixed with a partner registry in the repo. The lesson is worth keeping: building the matrix strictly from what is written down surfaced a real gap, and it also produced a wrong answer until I added what was not written down.

Finance accountability now splits with Danielle on accounts payable, month-end close and the annual budget, and me on vendor invoices, cost rollups and inventory valuation, since those flow out of purchasing and the inventory ledger.

Regulatory is the interesting one. Pedrero is consulted on eleven rows and responsible for none. That is not undervaluing them. Our own procedures make them consult only with no access to our systems, and Amy Pedrero holds the binding calls. The effect is a split bottleneck: Pedrero forms every regulatory opinion and I perform every regulatory action, because the Operator gate is mine and they cannot act inside Asana or PLM. Either that gate gets an alternate or their remit widens.

The bigger structural point: nine external parties hold execution across nine functions. Pedrero on regulatory, Ironclad on finance, Calm HR on people, WITHIN on digital marketing, Teknologics on the storefront, Coastal Interactive on back-end IT and equipment, plus Erin, Jan and Perrine. For a team of nine that is a sensible shape and it is how this surface gets covered at all. What it means is that continuity rests on nine engagements rather than on employment, and each one is a renewal decision rather than a retention conversation.
